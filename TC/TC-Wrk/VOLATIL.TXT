     Gerhard, the volatility you mention above is calculated in a number
     of different ways. The conventional method is to calculate the
     standard deviation of daily lognormal returns, expressed as an
     annualized percentage:

     Given a sample of closing prices O..N, first calculate the
     daily log returns:

     above, 260.0 is an approximation for the number of trading days
     in the year. The value of 'np' is the number of points in the
     price vector, 'vec'. N+1 points are required to calculate an
     N-day volatility measure.

     Various experts debate the proper number of days to use in arriving
     at a reasonable measure of current volatility, 20 seems a populaz
     number. Others debate on whether the actual number of days in a
     year should be used, instead of the number of trading days. Finally,
     there are other methods, known as "extreme value" calculations that
     take into account daily hiqhs and lows, instead of just looking at
     closing prices. All these methods are approximations on the market's
     actual volatility, which is an important input in pricing models.
